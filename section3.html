佛说阿弥陀经

舍利弗，于汝意云何？彼佛何故号阿弥陀？舍利弗，彼佛光明无量，照十方国，无所障碍，是故号为阿弥陀。又舍利弗，彼佛寿命，及其人民，无量无边阿僧祇劫，故名阿弥陀。舍利弗，阿弥陀佛成佛已来，于今十劫。又舍利弗，彼佛有无量无边声闻弟子，皆阿罗汉，非是算数之所能知；诸菩萨众，亦复如是。舍利弗，彼佛国土，成就如是功德庄严。又舍利弗，极乐国土，众生生者，皆是阿鞞跋致，其中多有一生补处，其数甚多，非是算数所能知之，但可以无量无边阿僧祇说。舍利弗，众生闻者，应当发愿，愿生彼国。所以者何？得与如是诸上善人俱会一处。舍利弗，不可以少善根福德因缘，得生彼国。舍利弗，若有善男子、善女人，闻说阿弥陀佛，执持名号，若一日、若二日、若三日、若四日、若五日、若六日、若七日，一心不乱，其人临命终时，阿弥陀佛与诸圣众，现在其前。是人终时，心不颠倒，即得往生阿弥陀佛极乐国土。舍利弗，我见是利，故说此言。若有众生，闻是说者，应当发愿，生阿弥陀佛国。

逐句解释

彼佛何故号阿弥陀？舍利弗，彼佛光明无量，照十方国，无所障碍，是故号为阿弥陀

佛陀问舍利弗：你知道那位佛为什么叫阿弥陀吗？「阿弥陀」是梵文 Amitābha 的音译，意思是「无量光」。阿弥陀佛的光明没有边际，照遍十方一切世界，没有任何东西能遮挡或阻碍。光明在佛教中象征智慧——无量光就是无边无际的智慧，遍照一切。

That Buddha's light is immeasurable, illuminating all lands in the ten directions without obstruction — this is why he is named Amitabha

The name Amitabha (阿弥陀) translates from Sanskrit as "Infinite Light" (无量光, Amitābha). The ten directions — north, south, east, west, the four intermediate directions, up, and down — represent totality, the entire universe in every dimension. Light that pervades all ten directions without obstruction is light that reaches everywhere, leaves nothing in darkness, encounters no barrier. In Buddhist symbolism, light represents wisdom: Amitabha's infinite light means infinite wisdom, the knowing that illuminates every corner of existence without exception. This is not merely a poetic description; it is a statement about Amitabha's nature — his awareness and compassion extend to every being in every realm, without limit and without preference.

又舍利弗，彼佛寿命，及其人民，无量无边阿僧祇劫，故名阿弥陀

阿弥陀的第二层含义是「无量寿」（梵文 Amitāyus）。阿弥陀佛的寿命，以及极乐世界众生的寿命，都是无量无边阿僧祇劫——「劫」是印度计算时间的最大单位，「阿僧祇」是梵文，意为无法计数的数量。简而言之：寿命无穷无尽，没有终结。

The lifespan of that Buddha and his people is immeasurable, boundless, and incalculable — this is why he is also named Amitabha

The second meaning of Amitabha is "Infinite Life" (无量寿, Amitāyus). A kalpa (劫) is an almost incomprehensibly vast unit of time in Indian cosmology — one common illustration describes it as the time it would take to wear down a mountain of stone by brushing it once every hundred years with a piece of silk. An asamkhya (阿僧祇) is a number so large it cannot be counted. Infinite asamkhya kalpas means, in plain terms: no end. In our world, all life is defined by its ending — illness, ageing, and death are unavoidable. The Pure Land turns this around: there is no ageing, no illness, and no unwanted end to life. Beings there have all the time they need to practise and awaken, without the pressure of mortality hanging over them. This is one of the most profound gifts of the Pure Land.

阿弥陀佛成佛已来，于今十劫

阿弥陀佛成佛到现在，已经有十劫之久。这说明阿弥陀佛是一位「现在佛」——他不是无始以来就是佛，而是在某个时刻通过修行成佛，极乐世界也是他成佛后建立的。十劫的跨度，对人类而言是无法想象的久远，但对于佛法而言，这说明极乐世界已经存在了极长时间，早已稳定成熟。

It has been ten kalpas since Amitabha Buddha attained Buddhahood

Ten kalpas is an unimaginably long span of time, yet it is not infinite — Amitabha had a beginning, and his Pure Land has existed for a specific (if vast) duration. This detail matters theologically. It confirms that Amitabha was once a practitioner who, through practice and the fulfilment of his vows, became a Buddha. His story is told in more detail in the longer Sukhavativyuha Sutra: he was once a king who became a monk named Dharmakara, made forty-eight vows before his teacher, and practised for aeons until every vow was fulfilled and his Pure Land was established. The ten kalpas also confirms that the Pure Land is not a new or untested destination — it has been fully active and welcoming beings for an immense period of time.

又舍利弗，彼佛有无量无边声闻弟子，皆阿罗汉，非是算数之所能知；诸菩萨众，亦复如是

阿弥陀佛有无数声闻弟子（已证阿罗汉果，断尽烦恼的圣者），以及同样无数的菩萨众。数量之多，无法用数字来表达。这说明极乐世界不是一个空旷孤寂的地方，而是一个充满了成就者的道场——修行者将在最优秀的同修中间修学。

That Buddha has immeasurable and boundless Shravaka disciples, all Arhats, beyond counting; and his assembly of Bodhisattvas is likewise

Shravakas (声闻, literally "hearers") are practitioners who attained Arhatship by hearing and following the Buddha's direct teachings. In the Pure Land, they are beyond numbering. The Bodhisattva assembly is equally vast. This detail addresses something any thoughtful person might wonder about the Pure Land: who will I be practising with? The answer is: the finest spiritual companions imaginable — Arhats who have completely overcome all defilements, and Bodhisattvas of immeasurable compassion and wisdom. The quality of one's companions is recognised across all Buddhist traditions as one of the most important factors in practice. The sutra will shortly make this explicit, encouraging beings to aspire for the Pure Land precisely because they will be "in one place together with all these excellent people" (诸上善人俱会一处).

极乐国土，众生生者，皆是阿鞞跋致，其中多有一生补处

生到极乐世界的众生，都是「阿鞞跋致」（梵文 avaivartika），意思是「不退转」——已经达到永远不会退堕、只会继续前进直到成佛的修行阶位。其中很多更是「一生补处」，即这一生结束后就能成佛，是最接近佛果的菩萨。这说明往生极乐世界的众生，修行成就之高令人难以想象。

All beings born in the land of Utmost Bliss are avaivartika — non-retrogressive — and among them many are in their last birth before Buddhahood

Avaivartika (阿鞞跋致, non-retrogressive) is a pivotal stage on the Bodhisattva path: once reached, the practitioner can never fall back to a lower level of realisation. Progress toward Buddhahood is irreversible. This is enormously significant for those who worry about backsliding in practice — in the Pure Land, that danger is permanently gone. Even more remarkable is that many residents are "one birth away from Buddhahood" (一生补处), the stage occupied by Maitreya in this world — meaning they need only one more lifetime before becoming fully enlightened Buddhas. The implication is striking: rebirth in the Pure Land is not a detour or a rest stop; it is, for most beings, the second-to-last step before Buddhahood itself.

众生闻者，应当发愿，愿生彼国。所以者何？得与如是诸上善人俱会一处。舍利弗，不可以少善根福德因缘，得生彼国

不能以少许的善根、福德和因缘就能往生极乐世界。往生需要足够的修行积累。但这句话紧接着的是执持名号的教导，说明「执持名号、一心不乱」本身就是具足善根福德的修行——念佛就是积累往生所需的资粮。

One cannot be born in that land with few roots of goodness, merit, and karmic conditions

This statement is sometimes misread as discouraging — as if the Pure Land is only for spiritual elites. But in context it functions differently. The Buddha is about to describe the practice that constitutes sufficient roots of goodness: holding the name of Amitabha with undivided attention. The point is that birth in the Pure Land requires genuine, wholehearted practice — not casual or half-hearted effort. At the same time, the practice itself (holding the name) is presented as available to anyone — male or female, monastic or lay, educated or not. The "roots of goodness and merit" are not accumulated over lifetimes of elaborate ritual; they are planted and grown through the single practice of sincere, focused recitation. The teaching is demanding in terms of quality of attention, but not in terms of social or educational prerequisites.

若有善男子、善女人，闻说阿弥陀佛，执持名号，若一日、若二日、若三日、若四日、若五日、若六日、若七日，一心不乱

这是整部经最核心的修行教导。善男子、善女人听闻阿弥陀佛的名号后，专心持念，从一天到七天，保持「一心不乱」。「执持名号」就是专注地念「阿弥陀佛」这四个字（或六字「南无阿弥陀佛」）。「一心不乱」是关键：心念集中在佛号上，不散乱，不间断。时间是一到七天，不是几十年，说明这是一个即生可以完成的修行。

If good men and good women hear of Amitabha Buddha and hold fast to his name — for one day, two days, three, four, five, six, or seven days — with an undivided and undistracted mind

This is the heart of the entire sutra. The practice is called nianfo (念佛) — "recollection of the Buddha" — and in the Pure Land tradition it means the sustained, devoted recitation of Amitabha's name: "Amituofo" (阿弥陀佛) or the fuller form "Namo Amituofo" (南无阿弥陀佛, meaning "I take refuge in Amitabha Buddha"). The instruction is precise: the recitation must be sustained (执持, "holding fast") and the mind must be 一心不乱 — literally "one mind, not in chaos." This means undivided attention: the name fills the mind completely, leaving no room for distraction, doubt, or wandering thought. The timeframe of one to seven days is striking in its accessibility — this is not a lifetime of monastic austerity but an intensive period of practice that any sincere person can undertake. At the same time, "one mind, undistracted" for even a single day is not trivial. It requires genuine effort, sincerity, and the willingness to let go of everything else.

其人临命终时，阿弥陀佛与诸圣众，现在其前。是人终时，心不颠倒，即得往生阿弥陀佛极乐国土

在临终的时刻，阿弥陀佛会与圣众一起出现在这个人面前。因为有佛与圣众现前加持，这人在最后关头心不散乱、不颠倒，就当下往生极乐世界。「心不颠倒」是往生的关键——临终时最难的就是保持清明，而佛的现前正是为了帮助行者在这一刻保持清明。这是阿弥陀佛四十八愿之一（第十八愿）的兑现。

When that person's life is drawing to its close, Amitabha Buddha will appear before them together with a host of holy beings. At the moment of passing, that person's mind will not be confused or inverted, and they will immediately be born in Amitabha Buddha's land of Utmost Bliss

This is the fulfilment of Amitabha's eighteenth vow — the most celebrated of his forty-eight vows — which promises that any being who sincerely recites his name will receive his welcome at the moment of passing. The phrase "mind not confused or inverted" (心不颠倒) is crucial. In Buddhist teaching, the state of mind at the moment of passing carries great weight — it shapes the conditions of the next rebirth. Ordinarily, the approach of death brings fear, confusion, pain, and regret — the mind is pulled in all directions and clarity is easily lost. For the sincere practitioner of nianfo, however, Amitabha appears in person. His presence steadies and clarifies the mind at its most vulnerable moment. The practice during life, and Amitabha's responsive presence at its close, work together: the practitioner has trained the mind to hold the name; Amitabha honours that training by arriving when it matters most.

舍利弗，我见是利，故说此言。若有众生，闻是说者，应当发愿，生阿弥陀佛国

佛陀说：舍利弗，我看到这个法门对众生有如此大的利益，所以主动说出来。凡是听到这个教说的众生，都应当发愿往生阿弥陀佛的国土。这里再次强调了这部经「无问自说」的特点：佛陀是因为见到众生的需要和利益，主动宣说，而不是被问到才说的。

Shariputra, I see this benefit, and therefore I speak these words. Any being who hears this teaching should vow to be born in the land of Amitabha Buddha

The Buddha closes this section by revealing his motivation: he speaks because he sees the benefit (利) this teaching brings to beings. This circles back to the "unprompted teaching" (无问自说) nature of the sutra noted in the introduction. Normally, Buddhist teachings arise in response to questions. Here, the Buddha volunteers the teaching out of compassion — he has something so important and beneficial to offer that he offers it without waiting to be asked. The instruction to "vow to be born there" (发愿，生阿弥陀佛国) is significant: in Pure Land Buddhism, the aspiration itself — the sincere wish to be reborn in the Pure Land — is a necessary component of the path, alongside faith (信) and practice (行). Together, faith, vow, and practice form the three essentials (三资粮) of the Pure Land path.

总结 · Summary

这一部分揭示了阿弥陀佛名号的两层含义——无量光与无量寿——并介绍了他的无量圣众。核心修行教导是：专心执持「阿弥陀佛」名号一至七日，保持一心不乱。如此修行者在临终时，阿弥陀佛会亲自现前接引，使其心不颠倒，当下往生极乐世界。这个法门的殊胜之处在于：它简单、直接、人人可行，而果报却是直接往生不退转的净土，乃至一生成佛。

This section introduces the two meanings of Amitabha's name — Infinite Light and Infinite Life — and describes his vast assembly of Arhats and Bodhisattvas. It then delivers the central practice instruction of the entire sutra: hold fast to the name of Amitabha Buddha for one to seven days with an undivided mind. The practitioner who does this sincerely will, at the close of life, receive Amitabha's personal welcome — steadying the mind at its most critical moment — and be immediately reborn in the Pure Land. What makes this teaching remarkable is its combination of simplicity and depth: the practice is available to anyone regardless of background or education, yet its result — rebirth among non-retrogressive Bodhisattvas in a realm perfectly designed for awakening — could not be more profound.
