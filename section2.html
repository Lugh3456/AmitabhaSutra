The Pure Land · 极乐世界

佛说阿弥陀经

← 第一章 目录 Contents 第三章 →

尔时，佛告长老舍利弗：「从是西方，过十万亿佛土，有世界名曰极乐。其土有佛，号阿弥陀，今现在说法。舍利弗，彼土何故名为极乐？其国众生，无有众苦，但受诸乐，故名极乐。又舍利弗，极乐国土，七重栏楯，七重罗网，七重行树，皆是四宝，周匝围绕，是故彼国名为极乐。又舍利弗，极乐国土，有七宝池，八功德水，充满其中，池底纯以金沙布地。四边阶道，金、银、琉璃、玻璃合成。其上有楼阁，亦以金、银、琉璃、玻璃、砗磲、赤珠、玛瑙而严饰之。池中莲花，大如车轮，青色青光，黄色黄光，赤色赤光，白色白光，微妙香洁。舍利弗，极乐国土，成就如是功德庄严。又舍利弗，彼佛国土，常作天乐，黄金为地，昼夜六时，雨天曼陀罗华。其土众生，常以清旦，各以衣裓，盛众妙华，供养他方十万亿佛，即以食时，还到本国，饭食经行。舍利弗，极乐国土，成就如是功德庄严。复次，舍利弗，彼国常有种种奇妙杂色之鸟：白鹤、孔雀、鹦鹉、舍利、迦陵频伽、共命之鸟。是诸众鸟，昼夜六时，出和雅音，其音演畅五根、五力、七菩提分、七圣道分，如是等法。其土众生，闻是音已，皆悉念佛、念法、念僧。舍利弗，汝勿谓此鸟实是罪报所生；所以者何？彼佛国土，无三恶道。舍利弗，其佛国土，尚无恶道之名，何况有实？是诸众鸟，皆是阿弥陀佛，欲令法音宣流，变化所作。舍利弗，彼佛国土，微风吹动，诸宝行树，及宝罗网，出微妙音，譬如百千种乐，同时俱作，闻是音者，自然皆生念佛、念法、念僧之心。舍利弗，其佛国土，成就如是功德庄严。」

逐句解释

尔时，佛告长老舍利弗：「从是西方，过十万亿佛土，有世界名曰极乐

佛陀告诉舍利弗：从我们这个世界向西，越过十万亿个佛国土，有一个世界叫做「极乐」。「十万亿」在这里表示极远的距离，并非精确的数字计算。佛土是一位佛所教化的世界范围，每个佛土都有其佛在主持教化。

To the west of here, past ten billion Buddha-lands, there is a world called Utmost Bliss

The Buddha now turns to Shariputra — his foremost disciple in wisdom — and begins the teaching. The Pure Land (极乐, Sukhavati in Sanskrit) lies to the west, beyond ten billion Buddha-lands (佛土). Each Buddha-land is the sphere of influence and teaching of one Buddha — a vast cosmos in its own right. Ten billion is not a literal measurement but an expression of immeasurable distance, placing the Pure Land firmly beyond the ordinary reach of human perception. "West" in Buddhist cosmology also carries symbolic resonance: the sun sets in the west, and the Pure Land is associated with the setting of the sun of life — the approach of death — and the new dawn of liberation that awaits beyond it. The name 极乐 means "utmost bliss" or "supreme happiness" — not a pleasure world but a realm entirely free from suffering.

其土有佛，号阿弥陀，今现在说法

极乐世界有一位佛，名号阿弥陀，正在那里说法。「今现在」这三个字非常重要：阿弥陀佛不是过去的古佛，不是传说，而是此时此刻就在那个世界活跃说法的佛。

In that land there is a Buddha named Amitabha, who right now is teaching the Dharma

Amitabha (阿弥陀, Sanskrit: Amitābha / Amitāyus) means both "Infinite Light" (无量光) and "Infinite Life" (无量寿). The phrase "right now is teaching the Dharma" (今现在说法) is theologically significant: Amitabha is not a historical figure from the distant past but a presently active Buddha. This makes the Pure Land an accessible destination — there is a living guide there, actively welcoming beings. In Mahayana cosmology, innumerable Buddhas exist simultaneously across countless worlds, each guiding beings toward awakening. Amitabha is one such Buddha, distinguished by his particular vows and by the qualities of the realm he has created.

其国众生，无有众苦，但受诸乐，故名极乐

为什么叫「极乐」？因为那个世界的众生没有任何苦，只有各种快乐。佛教将苦分为三类：苦苦（直接的痛苦）、坏苦（快乐消逝带来的苦）、行苦（无常变化本身带来的苦）。极乐世界三种苦都没有。

The beings of that land have no suffering at all; they experience only happiness — this is why it is called Utmost Bliss

Buddhism identifies three kinds of suffering: the suffering of pain (苦苦) — physical pain, illness, grief; the suffering of change (坏苦) — the heartache that comes when pleasure ends; and the pervasive suffering of conditioned existence (行苦) — the subtle unsatisfactoriness woven into all impermanent things, simply because they cannot last. In our world all three are unavoidable. The Pure Land is defined by the complete absence of all three. This is not described as a permanent heaven where beings rest forever, however — it is a realm of perfect conditions for practice, where all obstacles to awakening have been removed. The "bliss" is ultimately the bliss of an unobstructed path to liberation.

又舍利弗，极乐国土，七重栏楯，七重罗网，七重行树，皆是四宝，周匝围绕，是故彼国名为极乐

极乐世界有七重栏杆、七重宝网、七重宝树，都由金、银、琉璃、玻璃四种珍宝合成，环绕四周。「七重」象征完满与圆足，「四宝」象征纯净与珍贵。这不是在描述奢华的装饰，而是在表达那个世界的庄严与清净。

The land of Utmost Bliss is encircled by seven tiers of railings, seven layers of netting, and seven rows of trees, all composed of the four jewels

The number seven in Buddhist and Indian symbolism represents completeness — the seven directions, seven days, seven factors of enlightenment. Seven layers of protection and adornment signal that nothing is missing and nothing can harm. The four jewels (四宝) — gold (金), silver (银), lapis lazuli (琉璃), and crystal (玻璃) — represent the four cardinal directions and the full compass of purity. In Indian aesthetics, jewels symbolise not merely wealth but incorruptibility: they do not rot, rust, or decay. The image of a world built from indestructible, luminous materials is the sutra's way of expressing that the Pure Land itself is a manifestation of Amitabha's merit and enlightened intention — a world where impermanence has been overcome.

又舍利弗，极乐国土，有七宝池，八功德水，充满其中，池底纯以金沙布地

极乐世界有七宝莲池，池中充满具有八种功德的神圣之水。「八功德水」是指澄净、清冷、甘美、轻软、润泽、安和、除饥渴、长养诸根这八种特质。池底铺满金沙。一切都洁净、美好、滋养身心。

There are pools of seven jewels, filled with water endowed with eight meritorious qualities, and the beds of these pools are spread with golden sand

The eight meritorious qualities of the water (八功德水) are: clarity (澄净), coolness (清冷), sweetness (甘美), softness (轻软), nourishment (润泽), peacefulness (安和), ability to quench thirst and hunger (除饥渴), and ability to strengthen the faculties (长养诸根). Water in Indian spiritual symbolism is the medium of purification — it cleanses, sustains, and refreshes. Water with all eight qualities is perfect water, the kind that exists nowhere in our ordinary world but that perfectly supports practice and spiritual growth. The golden sand of the pool bed continues the theme of a world in which even the ground beneath one's feet is luminous and incorruptible.

四边阶道，金、银、琉璃、玻璃合成；其上有楼阁，亦以金、银、琉璃、玻璃、砗磲、赤珠、玛瑙而严饰之

莲池四周有由金、银、琉璃、玻璃构成的台阶和走道，池边矗立着用七种珍宝装饰的楼阁。「七宝」是佛教中常见的完整珍宝清单，代表从各个维度都达到圆满庄严。

The banks of the pools have stairways of gold, silver, lapis lazuli, and crystal; above them rise pavilions adorned with gold, silver, lapis lazuli, crystal, giant clam shell, red coral, and agate

The seven jewels (七宝) — gold, silver, lapis lazuli, crystal, giant clam shell (砗磲), red coral (赤珠), and agate (玛瑙) — appear frequently across Mahayana sutras as the complete set of precious materials. Each jewel has its own quality of light: gold glows warm; lapis lazuli shines deep blue; crystal is pure transparency. Together they produce an environment of multi-coloured, multi-textured luminosity. The pavilions (楼阁) suggest spaces of rest, contemplation, and teaching — structures perfectly suited to the life of practice. The overall image is of an environment that, in every direction and at every level, supports and celebrates the spiritual life rather than obstructing it.

池中莲花，大如车轮，青色青光，黄色黄光，赤色赤光，白色白光，微妙香洁

宝池中的莲花大如车轮，有青、黄、赤、白四色，各放出相应颜色的光芒，香气微妙，洁净无比。莲花在佛教中是最重要的象征之一：它从泥中生长，却出污泥而不染，正是修行者「处世而不染」的写照。

In the pools are lotus flowers as large as carriage wheels — blue lotuses radiating blue light, yellow lotuses yellow light, red lotuses red light, white lotuses white light — subtly fragrant and supremely pure

The lotus (莲花) is the defining symbol of Buddhism: it grows rooted in mud at the bottom of a pond yet rises above the water and blooms in perfect purity, untouched by the murk below. It is the image of the practitioner: living in the world with all its suffering and confusion, yet not defiled by it. In the Pure Land, these lotuses are enormous — as wide as a carriage wheel — and each radiates its own coloured light. The four colours correspond to four of the major Buddhas and Bodhisattvas in Mahayana iconography and to the four divine abodes (brahmaviharas): loving-kindness, compassion, sympathetic joy, and equanimity. The fragrance is described as "subtle" (微妙) — not overpowering, but refined, a quality that permeates without forcing itself.

又舍利弗，彼佛国土，常作天乐，黄金为地，昼夜六时，雨天曼陀罗华

极乐世界常有天上的音乐响起，大地是黄金铺成的，昼夜六时（即全天不间断）天降曼陀罗花雨。曼陀罗花（梵文 Māndārava）是天界的花，洁白芬芳，是吉祥的象征。整个环境时刻充满美妙与清净。

In that Buddha-land, celestial music plays constantly; the ground is of pure gold; and six times throughout the day and night, a rain of mandara flowers falls from the sky

The six periods of day and night (昼夜六时) is an Indian way of saying "at all times without ceasing" — the day was divided into three watches and the night into three, making six. Mandara flowers (曼陀罗华, Māndārava) are the coral tree flowers of the heavens, described across Buddhist texts as white, beautiful, and fragrant. A rain of flowers from the sky is the traditional Indian symbol of divine celebration — it is what heaven does when something wonderful happens. In the Pure Land, this happens constantly, not as a special event but as the ordinary condition of existence. Taken together with the golden ground and unceasing music, the image is of a world whose every element — sound, sight, touch of the earth beneath one's feet — is an expression of the Buddha's wisdom and compassion.

其土众生，常以清旦，各以衣裓，盛众妙华，供养他方十万亿佛，即以食时，还到本国，饭食经行

极乐世界的众生每天清晨用衣襟盛满美花，前往十方无数佛国供养诸佛，在吃饭的时间就已经回到了极乐世界，吃完饭后经行修习。这描述了一种极为自在、无碍的修行生活：供养、受食、经行，一切都在轻松圆满中完成。

Each morning, the beings of that land fill the folds of their robes with wondrous flowers and travel to make offerings to ten billion Buddhas in other lands, returning home in time for their meal, then walking in meditation

This passage gives us a glimpse of daily life in the Pure Land — and it is remarkable for how effortless it is. Beings cross unimaginable distances to offer flowers to countless Buddhas and return in time for breakfast. The ease of this describes a life in which all obstacles — of time, space, energy, and distraction — have been dissolved. Making offerings (供养) to Buddhas is a key Mahayana practice that cultivates generosity, reverence, and connection to the Dharma. Walking meditation (经行) is a standard form of mindfulness practice in all Buddhist traditions. The Pure Land does not abolish practice — it perfects the conditions for it. Every element of the practitioner's day is dedicated to awakening, without the friction of illness, poverty, distraction, or confusion that so often interrupts practice in our world.

复次，舍利弗，彼国常有种种奇妙杂色之鸟：白鹤、孔雀、鹦鹉、舍利、迦陵频伽、共命之鸟，昼夜六时，出和雅音，其音演畅五根、五力、七菩提分、七圣道分，如是等法

极乐世界有白鹤、孔雀、鹦鹉、舍利鸟、迦陵频伽（据说声音极美的鸟）、共命鸟（双头鸟）等各种奇妙的鸟，昼夜六时鸣唱和雅的声音，宣扬五根、五力、七觉支、八正道等佛法。听到这些声音的众生，自然生起念佛、念法、念僧的心。

That land is always filled with wondrous birds of many colours — white cranes, peacocks, parrots, sharikas, kalavinka birds, and jivanjivaka birds — who sing harmonious melodies throughout the day and night, their song proclaiming the Five Faculties, Five Powers, Seven Factors of Enlightenment, and the Noble Eightfold Path

The Five Faculties (五根) are faith, effort, mindfulness, concentration, and wisdom. The Five Powers (五力) are the same qualities developed to a strength that cannot be overwhelmed. The Seven Factors of Enlightenment (七菩提分) are mindfulness, investigation, effort, joy, tranquillity, concentration, and equanimity. The Noble Eightfold Path (七圣道分 / 八正道) is the Buddha's core teaching on right view, intention, speech, action, livelihood, effort, mindfulness, and concentration. These are the fundamental categories of Buddhist practice — the entire curriculum of awakening. In the Pure Land, this teaching is not only given by the Buddha in formal sessions; it is sung by birds, carried on the wind, woven into the very air. The environment itself is a Dharma teacher. The kalavinka (迦陵频伽) is a mythical bird said to sing with a voice more beautiful than any other creature; the jivanjivaka (共命之鸟) is a two-headed bird that shares one body — a symbol of the interdependence of all beings.

汝勿谓此鸟实是罪报所生；所以者何？彼佛国土，无三恶道。是诸众鸟，皆是阿弥陀佛，欲令法音宣流，变化所作

佛陀特别解释：不要以为这些鸟是由罪业感召而生的动物（因为在我们的世界，投生为动物被认为是恶报）。极乐世界没有三恶道，连「恶道」这个名字都没有，更何况有实际的恶道。这些鸟都是阿弥陀佛为了广传法音而化现的，不是真正的动物众生。

Do not imagine that these birds are born of karmic retribution. In that Buddha-land, the three lower realms do not even exist in name, let alone in fact. These birds are all transformations created by Amitabha Buddha to make the sound of the Dharma flow everywhere

In Buddhist cosmology, rebirth as an animal is considered an unfavourable state arising from unwholesome karma. A careful listener might therefore wonder: are the birds of the Pure Land suffering in animal bodies? The Buddha directly addresses this concern. The three lower realms (三恶道) — hell, the hungry ghost realm, and the animal realm — do not exist in the Pure Land; even their names are absent. There is no suffering of that kind there. The birds are not sentient beings serving out karmic sentences; they are manifestations of Amitabha's intention — created forms through which the wisdom of the Dharma is broadcast. This reveals something important about the nature of the Pure Land: everything in it, including the birds, the trees, the wind, and the water, is an expression of Amitabha's enlightened activity. The Pure Land is not a natural environment that happens to be pleasant; it is itself a teaching.

彼佛国土，微风吹动，诸宝行树，及宝罗网，出微妙音，譬如百千种乐，同时俱作，闻是音者，自然皆生念佛、念法、念僧之心

极乐世界里，微风轻拂七宝树林和宝罗网，发出微妙的声音，好像百千种乐器同时演奏。凡是听到这声音的人，自然会生起念佛、念法、念僧（即三宝）的心。环境本身就是修行的助力。

When a gentle breeze moves through the jewelled trees and jewelled nets, a subtle and wondrous sound arises — as if a hundred thousand instruments were playing all at once. Everyone who hears it naturally gives rise to the thought of the Buddha, the Dharma, and the Sangha

The Three Jewels (三宝) — Buddha, Dharma, and Sangha — are the central objects of refuge in Buddhism. To "give rise to the thought of" the Three Jewels (念佛、念法、念僧) is to turn the mind toward awakening — the foundational act of Buddhist practice. In our world, practitioners must make deliberate effort to remember the Three Jewels amidst the constant noise and distraction of ordinary life. In the Pure Land, this effort is made unnecessary by the environment itself: even the sound of the wind through the trees is a reminder. This is the deeper meaning of 极乐 — utmost bliss. It is not pleasure in the ordinary sense but the bliss of a world in which every element supports, rather than obstructs, the path to liberation. The Dharma is not confined to books or formal sessions; it arises spontaneously from every corner of existence.

总结 · Summary

这一部分是对极乐世界的详细描述。佛陀告诉舍利弗：极乐国土由七宝构成，有宝池、莲花、天乐、花雨，有宣扬佛法的奇妙鸟类和自然发出法音的宝树。整个世界没有任何苦，没有三恶道，一切——包括风声、鸟声——都在引导众生念佛、念法、念僧。这并非文学上的描绘，而是在说：那是一个把修行的一切障碍都清除了的完美道场。

This section presents the Buddha's detailed description of the Pure Land. Every element — the jewelled railings, the lotus pools with their eight-virtue water, the flower rains, the singing birds, the wind through the jewelled trees — serves one purpose: to make the arising of enlightenment effortless. In our world, practitioners fight constant obstacles: illness, poverty, distraction, forgetfulness, bad company, noisy environments. In the Pure Land, the environment itself becomes the teacher. Even the wind carries the Dharma. The extraordinary sensory richness of this description is not an appeal to materialism — it is a way of saying that in the Pure Land, no moment and no sense impression is wasted. Everything points toward awakening.
